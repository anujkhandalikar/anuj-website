One thing I have been quietly doing on the side — running a small community experiment called Noticing Beauty. The premise is embarrassingly simple: find one beautiful thing a day. Post about it. Do it together.
I started it because I noticed I was spending a lot of time consuming things — reels, feeds, noise — and very little time actually seeing. Not looking, seeing. The kind of seeing where you stop mid-walk because light hit something just right. A weird crack in a wall that looks like a map. An old man's hands. Condensation on glass.
So I put out a WhatsApp group. Invited some people. Said — 30 days, one beautiful thing a day, no format rules. A blurry photo counts. A one-liner counts. The beautiful thing matters, the packaging doesn't.
We grew to about 50 people. People loved it more than I expected. I think because it's not productivity. It's not self-improvement. It's just — attention. Trained slowly, together. Humans are wired for pessimism — survival instinct and all that. But we're way past survival. Noticing beauty is how I remind myself of that.
Chotu now handles the daily posting to the group when I am unavailable. Which feels right — the experiment continues even when I step away. That's kind of the point.
